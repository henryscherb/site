03 · Neurotrauma Behavioral Health Startup
Amoji · Co-founder & CEO · Summer 2023–2024

Neurotrauma changes more than one life. We built tools for everyone it touches.

Amoji is a behavioral health startup I co-founded to support people living with neurotrauma, and their families and friends, through each phase of recovery. We validated the need with 350+ interviews, piloted two iOS products, and designed a third for the long term. As CEO, I set direction and scope for a 15-person product team alongside my co-founder and CPO, Joanne Chuang.

Status: Paused at the end of 2024 after I joined Hayden full-time. The lessons shaped my work at BreakThru SCI.
The insight

The market for neurotrauma's behavioral health impact is much larger than the number of people injured, because the impact spreads to everyone close to them.

After my brother's spinal cord injury, I watched it ripple through his relationships: how he related to others, and how they related to him. I tested that idea before building anything, through discovery interviews with 350+ people with SCI, family members, friends, therapists and researchers.

One product for each phase of recovery

So the company could stay with a community from the ICU onward.

  1. Phase 1 · ICU and acute

    Send Amoji

    MVP · Piloted

    A care page where friends send one-tap emoji support, the community's "amoji," with links to meals, donations and cards. Support without the pressure of finding the perfect words.

    Result: 5,000+ amoji sent in our pilot.
  2. Phase 2 · Subacute

    Promptly

    iOS Messages extension · Piloted

    Adaptive, psychologically informed prompts inside group chats, mixing light-hearted, deeper and group-chosen topics to keep friends and family talking.

    Result: 75+ pilot users reported much stronger feelings of connection.
  3. Phase 3 · Chronic

    Moova

    Preliminary design · Pipeline

    A Strava-style social fitness platform that celebrates effort, not activity type, so people with neurological impairments and their friends can share movement together.

    Result: Designed as the next product after product-market fit.
